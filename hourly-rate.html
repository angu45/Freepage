Why your freelance rate is usually too low

The "Hidden Costs"

Unlike employees, you pay for your own laptop, software (Adobe, Slack, AI tools), health insurance, and office space. If you don't add these to your rate, you are paying your client to work for them.

The "Tax Trap"

The income you calculate here is Gross Revenue. Depending on your country, you should set aside 15% to 35% of every dollar you earn for income tax and self-employment tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many billable hours can I actually work? ▼
Most freelancers can only sustain 20 to 25 billable hours per week. The rest of your 40-hour week is spent on marketing, sending invoices, and finding new clients.
Should I charge by the hour or by the project? ▼
Hourly rates are great for ongoing support. However, for specific projects, "Value-Based Pricing" is often better. Use this calculator to find your *minimum* base rate, then quote projects based on the value delivered to the client.
